Why the paired column and not the intervals

Every system answers the same 470 questions, so the comparison is paired and the right test is exact McNemar over the discordant pairs — the questions where exactly one of the two systems was right. The marginal 95% intervals in the table treat two paired samples as independent, which is why they overlap almost by construction and why they cannot settle a difference of a few dozen questions. Read the intervals as the precision of each row on its own, and the paired column as the comparison.

Read this with the number

The lead is category-shaped, not general. Against hybrid_rag it is +7.0 on knowledge-update, +11.5 on multi-session and +7.1 on temporal — the three categories where the answer depends on which version of a fact is current, or on connecting sessions. On single-session recall, where there is no supersession to get right, the two are level (0.911 vs 0.911) and bm25’s 0.929 beats both. On single-session preference we score 0.167, the worst number on the board, and hybrid_rag beats us there. And we lose LoCoMo (section 07). The ledger is not a better retriever; it is a defence against confidently returning a value that stopped being true.

single_session_preference at 0.167 is the clearest open weakness in the system. Preference questions ask for a rubric-shaped answer — “what advice would suit me?” — that no attribute lookup helps with, and the fact block spends tokens the excerpts needed. Nothing in the design addresses that, and no fix is claimed here.

Full context collapsing to 0.162 at 31,531 tokens is the sharpest version of the long-context result this project keeps reproducing. On the oracle haystack the same system scores 0.519 — a figure from the batch-judged run in section 04, so the two are indicative rather than strictly comparable. Add the distractors a real user’s history would contain and it loses two thirds of its accuracy while costing 32× the tokens.

03The audit

An outside audit was asked to refute the previous headline, and it did.

The reviewer’s brief was to break the result, not to confirm it. Four findings landed. Two of them invalidate comparisons that had already been published, which is why the tables further down this page are marked superseded instead of quietly restated. The fourth is not fixed, cannot be fixed by editing code, and applies to every p-value on this page including the one in section 02.

What the audit found, what it did to the numbers, and whether it is fixed
finding what it did to the numbers status
Batched judging contaminated verdicts across systems Eight (question, gold, answer) triples went into one judge call and the batches mixed systems, so changing one system’s answer changed the prompt around a different system’s unchanged answer and could flip its verdict. Demonstrated on our own artifacts: two hybrid_rag questions with byte-identical answers were judged differently across two runs, which is that system’s entire 0.708 → 0.736 movement. Fixed. Judging is one question per call by default, groups are per-system and sorted by question id, and the run records which mode it used. Every table below was produced before this and is not comparable to section 02.
The never-empty retrieval fallback was a future-information leak When the time-bounded retrieval pass came back empty, retrieval was re-run without the cutoff. Answering “what did I believe as of March” with something the store learned in April is the exact failure this design exists to prevent, and labelling it did not help, because the answering model is still told to answer from what it was handed. Fixed by deletion. The real problem underneath it — 14 of 127 LongMemEval temporal questions are dated before every session in their own haystack — is a broken dataset field, now repaired in the adapter, which drops a bound that would exclude the entire haystack and records that it did.
The computed-time block reasoned over evidence the model could not see It took dates from every retrieved fact, including ones the fact budget had dropped, so it could state a span between two records only one of which appeared in the context — a number the reader had no way to falsify. Fixed. Only rendered facts can define a span, both endpoints are named, and the line says the pair may not be the pair asked about. A test pins it: at a tight budget the second fact is dropped and no span is stated.
Shipped constants were tuned on the benchmark’s own questions GRAPH_EXCERPT_SHARE, MAX_DATED_ITEMS and the hybrid lexical weight were each chosen by sweeping on LoCoMo and LongMemEval proxies. LongMemEval’s oracle and s variants carry the same 500 questions, so a constant tuned on oracle is tuned on the questions s is then scored on. That is test-set tuning. Not fixed. A held-out split is the fix and it has not been done.

The finding that is not fixed, stated plainly

Because several shipped constants were selected by sweeping on the benchmark’s own questions, every p-value on this page is post-selection rather than confirmatory — including p = 0.033 in section 02. It is a milder version of writing gold answers into a prompt, and it is the same family of problem. The fix is a held-out split, it has not been done, and until it is, the honest reading of the paired test is that it was computed on data the constants had already seen.

Two smaller items from the same audit are worth stating because they moved artifacts rather than opinions. Result files now record provenance — commit, dirty flag, judge mode and a manifest digest over the exact claim list every system received — so “every system saw the same claims” is checkable instead of asserted; files written before 2026-08-15 carry none of that. And the original knowledge-update table claimed a significance it never had: paired McNemar on that same artifact is 16 won / 9 lost, p = 0.23. That correction was made in place rather than by withdrawing the table.

Where it loses

Full context wins LoCoMo outright, at 23× the tokens. Among budget-matched systems, Palimpsest 0.408 and BM25 0.417 are a statistical tie — intervals almost entirely overlapping. Palimpsest leads on multi-hop and temporal; BM25 leads on single-hop and open-domain. If your workload looks like LoCoMo, use BM25.

Why, and why that is the expected result

LoCoMo is dominated by single-hop recall of details inside an utterance — “what did the charity race raise awareness for?” A fact ledger has nothing to offer that question, and the fact block spends budget that would otherwise buy excerpts.

Two things in the table are worth more than our own row:

  • Full context wins outright at 0.549, 13.2 points above the best budget-matched system (bm25, 0.417) and 14.1 above Palimpsest, at 23× the tokens. If you can afford to put the transcript in the prompt on every turn, on this benchmark you should. Vendors reporting LoCoMo rarely show this row.
  • Fact-layer memory is far worse than raw retrieval: mem0_style 0.237 and zep_style 0.278, against BM25’s 0.417, on the identical extracted claims. This corroborates the LightMem reproduction (arXiv 2607.29104) — memory construction destroys 11.3 points. Discarding source utterances in favour of a fact list loses more than the structure recovers: those two sit 18.0 and 13.9 points below plain BM25. Palimpsest is 17.1 points above mem0_style and 13.0 above zep_style because it keeps the utterances and the ledger — while still not beating BM25 here.

A caution that applies to our own earlier numbers

On a 3-conversation subset BM25 led by 14 points. At full scale that gap closes to 0.9 points. Small-subset benchmarking is not a preview of the full result, and this project published a 3-conversation figure before it had the full one.

09Corrections

Fixing our own bug cost 9.5 points, and that is on this page.

On the LoCoMo 3-conversation subset, Palimpsest scored 0.502 before the audit, with temporal at 0.629. After fixing the cardinality defect: 0.407, temporal 0.514.

The pre-fix score was higher because the bug created supersessions that should not have existed — a claim mislabelled multi skipped interval repair, and the resulting chaos happened to produce “EARLIER VALUES” blocks that flattered the temporal category. Part of the apparent advantage was an artifact of a broken ledger. The lower number is the true one.

The commands marked superseded reproduce the file, not the batched judging that produced the published numbers: re-running them now judges one question per call, which is the point of re-running them. 11Summary

On LongMemEval it is first. On LoCoMo it is not.

  • LongMemEval-S, all six categories, 470 questions, full distractors: first at 0.519, ahead of hybrid RAG (0.472), BM25 (0.430) and full context (0.162 at 32× the tokens). First on four of six categories, level on a fifth, and behind hybrid RAG on single-session preference, where our 0.167 is the worst number on the board. This is the one result produced under the corrected harness, and the margin over the runner-up clears a paired test — 60 won / 38 lost, p = 0.033 — which no lead in this project had ever done before.
  • LongMemEval oracle, all six categories: first at 0.589, but judged in batches and awaiting re-run, and its margin over the runner-up is not significant.
  • LoCoMo, all 10 conversations: full context wins at 23× the tokens, and among budget-matched systems Palimpsest and BM25 are a statistical tie. That is a loss and it is not qualified by anything above.

In the corrected 470-question run every margin is significant on a paired test, including the one over the runner-up. In every table produced before that run, no margin over the runner-up was significant, and those tables say so where they appear — a rank, not a demonstrated difference.

One caveat outranks all of them and it is not fixed: several shipped constants were tuned by sweeping on the benchmark’s own questions, which makes every p-value on this page post-selection rather than confirmatory. A held-out split is the fix and has not been done.

If your workload is factoid recall over a transcript and you can afford the tokens, put the transcript in the prompt. If your agent’s problem is that it keeps confidently telling users things that stopped being true — and that it cannot tell you what it believed last month — that is what this is for.

Palimpsest

An open-world bitemporal claim-interval memory engine for AI agents. A palimpsest is a manuscript scraped and rewritten, where the earlier text stays legible underneath. So is this.
