01Setup

What was held constant, stated before any score.

Every free parameter that moves a score more than an architecture does — averaging, denominator, judge choice, dataset version, token budget — is fixed here and printed with the numbers. It is the only reason these seven rows can be compared to each other. It is also why they cannot be stacked against a published figure that omits them.

Harness configuration, identical for every system in every table on this page
parameter value
Answering model claude-haiku
Judge model claude-haiku — a separate call from the answerer
Judge prompt Mem0 formulation (arXiv 2504.19413), unmodified
Averaging micro (question-weighted)
Token budget 1,024 for every retrieval system
Full-context budget 32,000 — an upper-bound reference, not budget-matched
LongMemEval data cleaned release (the widely-cited one was deprecated 2025-09-19)
Canonicalization seed synonyms + deterministic guards, no LLM, unless a row says otherwise
Extraction one shared LLM pass; every fact-based system gets the identical claims

Holding extraction constant is the point. Palimpsest, the Mem0-style flat fact layer and the Zep-style temporal graph receive the same claims and differ only in what they do with them, so any gap between those three is attributable to the storage semantics rather than to somebody’s extractor prompt.

Read this with every number below

In both LongMemEval tables the confidence interval overlaps the runner-up, so the margin over second place is not statistically significant. The margin over BM25 and below is. And we lose LoCoMo.

Where it loses

Full context wins LoCoMo outright, at 23× the tokens. Among budget-matched systems, Palimpsest 0.408 and BM25 0.417 are a statistical tie — intervals almost entirely overlapping. Palimpsest leads on multi-hop and temporal; BM25 leads on single-hop and open-domain. If your workload looks like LoCoMo, use BM25.

Why, and why that is the expected result

LoCoMo is dominated by single-hop recall of details inside an utterance — “what did the charity race raise awareness for?” A fact ledger has nothing to offer that question, and the fact block spends budget that would otherwise buy excerpts.

Two things in the table are worth more than our own row:

  • Full context wins outright at 0.549, 13.2 points above the best budget-matched system (bm25, 0.417) and 14.1 above Palimpsest, at 23× the tokens. If you can afford to put the transcript in the prompt on every turn, on this benchmark you should. Vendors reporting LoCoMo rarely show this row.
  • Fact-layer memory is far worse than raw retrieval: mem0_style 0.237 and zep_style 0.278, against BM25’s 0.417, on the identical extracted claims. This corroborates the LightMem reproduction (arXiv 2607.29104) — memory construction destroys 11.3 points. Discarding source utterances in favour of a fact list loses more than the structure recovers: those two sit 18.0 and 13.9 points below plain BM25. Palimpsest is 17.1 points above mem0_style and 13.0 above zep_style because it keeps the utterances and the ledger — while still not beating BM25 here.

A caution that applies to our own earlier numbers

On a 3-conversation subset BM25 led by 14 points. At full scale that gap closes to 0.9 points. Small-subset benchmarking is not a preview of the full result, and this project published a 3-conversation figure before it had the full one.

Is the ledger doing the work, or the retriever?

Retrieval ceiling — gold answer present in context, an LLM-free proxy over 383 LoCoMo questions — varying only the share of the token budget the structured fact block may take.

fact-block share overall single-hop temporal
0.35 21.9% 34.0% 10.0%
0.15 (chosen) 22.7% 35.0% 10.0%
0.10 22.5% 35.5% 7.8%
0.00 (no ledger) 21.7% 35.0% 5.6%

n = 383 · no answering model and no judge — this measures whether the gold answer is present in the assembled context, which is a ceiling, not an accuracy. It is reported as a ceiling and never placed beside an answer-accuracy number.

Removing the fact block costs almost nothing overall and halves the temporal category. The ledger is not carrying the general case; it carries temporal questions specifically, which is exactly the claim.

+3.5 points, at one LLM call per never-before-seen predicate surface form. It is off by default because a LongMemEval-S episode carries around 240 claims and the calls made a single benchmark run take hours — which would put the numbers out of reach of anyone trying to reproduce them offline. Every headline number on this page is the no-LLM configuration, so the LongMemEval result does not depend on it.

Predicate canonicalization, measured directly

No vendor in this space publishes a number for this, so there is nothing to compare against. Measured on the 123 predicate surface forms an LLM extractor actually emitted over LoCoMo, against 103 hand-labelled gold clusters.

Predicate clustering, 123 emitted surface forms against 103 hand-labelled gold clusters
configuration precision recall F1
guards only, no LLM 0.778 0.111 0.194

Gold clusters, the labelling rule and the trap set are in bench/canon_labels.json; the run is results/canon_guards_only.json. Recall of 0.111 is low and is printed here rather than omitted.

Precision is weighted far above recall by design: a false merge destroys facts — merge favorite_food with least_favorite_food and the ledger believes the user’s favourite food became the thing they hate — while a missed merge only costs a little retrieval recall.

07Corrections

Fixing our own bug cost 9.5 points, and that is on this page.

On the LoCoMo 3-conversation subset, Palimpsest scored 0.502 before the audit, with temporal at 0.629. After fixing the cardinality defect: 0.407, temporal 0.514.

The pre-fix score was higher because the bug created supersessions that should not have existed — a claim mislabelled multi skipped interval repair, and the resulting chaos happened to produce “EARLIER VALUES” blocks that flattered the temporal category. Part of the apparent advantage was an artifact of a broken ledger. The lower number is the true one.

Palimpsest before and after the cardinality fix, LoCoMo 3-conversation subset
measurement before the audit after the fix change
overall accuracy 0.502 0.407 −9.5
temporal category 0.629 0.514 −11.5

3-conversation subset, same harness and models both times. Change is in accuracy points. Nine defects were found in this engine and its harness in total, including one where it could see the future and inflate its own results. Every number on this page was re-run after those fixes; the pre-fix artifacts were withdrawn rather than corrected.

08Reproduce

Run it yourself.

Every table above comes from one runner. The raw per-question JSON for each is committed, so any row can be re-aggregated without re-running anything.

shell
./scripts/fetch_data.sh

# Section 02 and 03 — LongMemEval, all six categories, oracle
python -m bench.run --dataset longmemeval --variant oracle --all \
    --systems palimpsest,hybrid_rag,vector_rag,bm25,mem0_style,zep_style,full_context \
    --out results/final/lme_oracle_all_categories.json

# Section 04 — knowledge-update with ~500 distractor sessions per question
python -m bench.run --dataset longmemeval --variant s --all \
    --categories knowledge_update \
    --systems palimpsest,hybrid_rag,vector_rag,bm25,mem0_style,zep_style,full_context \
    --out results/final/lme_s_knowledge_update.json

# Section 05 — LoCoMo, all 10 conversations
python -m bench.run --dataset locomo --all \
    --systems palimpsest,hybrid_rag,vector_rag,bm25,mem0_style,zep_style,full_context \
    --out results/final/locomo_10conv.json

Defaults that the commands above rely on: --budget 1024 and --model haiku. full_context overrides the budget to 32,000 by design, because comparing it at 1,024 tokens would measure the truncation rather than the method — a rigged baseline is not a win.

09Summary

On LongMemEval it is first. On LoCoMo it is not.

  • LongMemEval, all six categories, 470 questions: first at 0.589, ahead of hybrid RAG (0.553) and of full context (0.536, at 5.7× the tokens), and first on four of six categories including the two hardest for everyone.
  • LongMemEval-S knowledge-update, with realistic distractors: first at 0.736, +9.7 over BM25 and +34.7 over full context on 1/32 of the tokens. It is also the system that degrades least when distractors are introduced (−1.4 points, against hybrid RAG’s −5.6 and full context’s −29.2).
  • LoCoMo, all 10 conversations: full context wins at 23× the tokens, and among budget-matched systems Palimpsest and BM25 are a statistical tie.

In both interval-overlap cases the margin over the runner-up is not significant at these sample sizes, and that is stated wherever the number appears.

If your workload is factoid recall over a transcript and you can afford the tokens, put the transcript in the prompt. If your agent’s problem is that it keeps confidently telling users things that stopped being true — and that it cannot tell you what it believed last month — that is what this is for.

Palimpsest

An open-world bitemporal claim-interval memory engine for AI agents. A palimpsest is a manuscript scraped and rewritten, where the earlier text stays legible underneath. So is this.
